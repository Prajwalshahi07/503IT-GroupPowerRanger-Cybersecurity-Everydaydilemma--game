Prototype Description: Everyday Dilemma
this is what our group did in last 3 weeks
Front Page (Welcome Page)
The front page is slightly decorated and features the game logo alongside the prominent title “Everyday Dilemma”. Below the title are three neon-coloured buttons (part of the cyberpunk neon theme): HOW TO PLAY, PLAY, and SCORES. Each button leads to a dedicated page.
A new user registration feature has been added directly on the home page. Players must register before accessing the game. Once registered, Mission 1 unlocks immediately.
HOW TO PLAY Page
This page clearly explains the full game objective and rules with a smooth delayed pop-up effect on the instructions for better engagement.
Objective
Identify phishing emails and texts, then choose the safest option to protect your digital identity and “pass the semester safely.”
How the Game Works

You play as a Coventry University student facing three realistic missions based on common phishing scams university students actually encounter.
Each mission presents a visual phishing scenario (email or text message) using realistic images and interfaces that simulate real-world cyberattack techniques: urgency, fear, and fake authority.
You have a limited time to analyse the message and choose between three possible actions.
Safe → highest points + positive feedback
Risky → partial points
Dangerous → point deduction + explanation of consequences

After every choice, an automatic pop-up appears with:
Immediate feedback on your decision
Educational explanation of phishing red flags (suspicious links, fake domains, urgency tactics, etc.)
Learning objectives and tips to improve your cybersecurity awareness

The decision-making system tracks your score dynamically. Correct/safe choices advance you smoothly; risky or dangerous choices reduce your score and show the potential real-life impact.
Missions are unlocked sequentially:
Mission 1: Urgent assignment re-upload phishing scam
Mission 2: Fake university IT password reset scam
Mission 3: Fake tuition fee refund phishing scam


You can only unlock the next mission after successfully completing the current one.
Game Progression & Ending

The score system continuously updates your cybersecurity awareness performance.
After finishing all three missions, a final results screen summarises your total score and overall performance.
Different endings appear based on your final score:
High score → “Safe Semester Achieved” + “Phishing Defender” recognition
Lower score → warning messages about compromised cybersecurity awareness

If you make too many dangerous choices, the attacker “hacks” your details and you fail to pass the semester safely. Safe choices let you graduate safely.
You can replay the game anytime to improve your score and knowledge.

The entire experience combines education and gamification to make phishing awareness training engaging and memorable for university students. The visual design uses a consistent cyberpunk neon theme for an immersive cybersecurity atmosphere.
The project is built with HTML (structure), CSS (styling and neon effects), and JavaScript (game logic, scoring, progression, pop-ups, and feedback system).
This combined prototype merges the original welcome-page layout and core gameplay concept from the first version with all the improvements from the second version (registration, mission structure, detailed scenarios, visual realism, scoring system, sequential unlocking, instant educational feedback, and varied endings) into one polished, complete experience.
