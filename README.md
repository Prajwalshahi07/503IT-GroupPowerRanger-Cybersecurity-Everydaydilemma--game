# Everyday Dilemma – Cyber Phishing Awareness Game

## Project Overview

**Everyday Dilemma** is an educational cybersecurity awareness game prototype developed by a professional development team to educate university students about phishing attacks and digital safety. The game is designed for students aged 18–25 and combines storytelling, gamification, and interactive decision-making to create an engaging learning experience. Through realistic scenarios, players learn how to recognise phishing attempts, avoid social engineering tactics, and practice safer online behaviour.

The project was built using HTML for structure, CSS for styling and animations, and JavaScript for gameplay logic, scoring systems, mission progression, and interactive educational feedback.

# Week 1 – Planning & Concept Development

During the first week, the team focused on planning and developing the overall concept of the game. Discussions were held to finalise the game title, cybersecurity topic, target audience, storyline ideas, learning objectives, and gameplay structure. After reviewing several cybersecurity issues, the team selected phishing awareness as the central educational theme because of its relevance to university students and increasing online threats.

The game concept follows an international university student living abroad independently and facing realistic online challenges. Players experience situations involving suspicious emails, fake university messages, and phishing scams that require careful decision-making. One of the early scenarios involved a fake assignment resubmission email threatening visa cancellation if immediate action was not taken. Players had to decide whether to click the suspicious link or verify the information through the official university website.

The team also established the game’s scoring system during this stage. Safe decisions reward players with higher points, while risky or dangerous actions reduce the score and trigger educational explanations. Players who achieve a score of 70 or above successfully “pass the semester safely,” while lower scores lead to negative in-game consequences such as compromised accounts or academic setbacks.

The educational objective of the game was designed to help students recognise phishing emails, understand social engineering tactics, identify suspicious links, and learn the real-world consequences of sharing sensitive information online.

# Session 2 – UX Research & Design Inspiration

In the second session, the team conducted research into existing cybersecurity awareness games to improve the project’s user experience and visual design. Inspiration was taken from games such as CyberMission, Prodigy Education, and CyberEdu. From these references, the team adopted a cyber military atmosphere, clearer visual indicators, and high-contrast colour schemes to improve engagement and accessibility.

This research helped shape the game’s final cyberpunk neon theme, which became the consistent visual style across all pages and gameplay sections. The storyboard and project canvas were also updated to reflect the improved user interface and gameplay flow.

---

# Week 2 – Prototype Development

During Week 2, the team focused on developing the first functional prototype of the game. The welcome page was designed with a decorated cyberpunk neon layout featuring the game title “Everyday Dilemma,” the project logo, and three main navigation buttons: HOW TO PLAY, PLAY, and SCORES. Each button directed users to a dedicated section of the game.

A registration system was also added to the home page so that players must register before accessing the missions. Once registered, Mission 1 becomes unlocked immediately, allowing players to begin the game experience.

The HOW TO PLAY section was then developed with animated pop-up effects and smooth transitions to make the instructions more engaging and easier to follow. This section clearly explains the game objective, rules, scoring system, and mission progression.

---

# Week 3 – Gameplay Expansion & Feature Integration

During the third week, the team expanded the gameplay system and integrated the main educational and interactive features into the prototype. The game was developed around three realistic phishing missions designed to reflect common scams faced by university students in real life.

Mission 1 focused on an urgent assignment re-upload phishing scam, Mission 2 presented a fake university IT password reset attack, and Mission 3 involved a fake tuition fee refund phishing scam. Each mission used realistic email and text message simulations that included urgency, fear tactics, suspicious links, and fake authority to replicate genuine phishing attempts.

Players were given limited time to analyse each scenario and choose between safe, risky, or dangerous responses. The scoring system updated dynamically based on player decisions, rewarding safe choices while reducing points for risky actions. Missions were unlocked sequentially, requiring players to successfully complete one mission before progressing to the next.

An educational feedback system was also fully integrated during this stage. After every decision, automatic pop-up messages appeared explaining the consequences of the player’s choice and highlighting phishing red flags such as fake domains, suspicious URLs, impersonation attempts, and urgency tactics. These explanations were designed to reinforce cybersecurity awareness and improve learning outcomes through interactive gameplay.

The team also completed the final results and ending system. After all missions were completed, players received a total score summary and performance evaluation. High-scoring players achieved the “Safe Semester Achieved” ending and earned “Phishing Defender” recognition, while lower scores resulted in warnings about compromised cybersecurity awareness and unsafe online behaviour.

By the end of Week 3, the project had evolved into a complete and polished prototype that combined realistic phishing simulations, educational feedback, mission progression, gamification, and immersive cyberpunk neon visuals into one cohesive experience.

# Week 4 – Testing & Finalisation

During the first day of Week 4, the team carried out complete testing of the final prototype. This included gameplay testing, user interface checks, score validation, mission progression verification, and testing animations and pop-up systems. The team identified several bugs and minor issues during testing and worked collaboratively to fix and optimise the final product.

After debugging and refinement, the game was finalised successfully. The completed project demonstrates how gamification and interactive storytelling can effectively educate university students about phishing attacks and cybersecurity awareness in an engaging and memorable way.
